Building Psychological Safety

Culture · 22 min · Q2 2026

📖 Learn
🎬 Video

CARD 1 OF 3

1. What is psychological safety?

Psychological safety, a term popularized by Harvard professor Amy Edmondson, is the shared belief among team members that the group is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. It means you can speak up, ask questions, make mistakes, and offer ideas without fear of humiliation, punishment, or rejection. Research across 180 teams at Google found that psychological safety was the single most important factor distinguishing high-performing teams from average ones.

💡 Safety to speak up is the #1 driver of team performance.

CARD 2 OF 3

2. Why it matters in the Kenyan workplace

In many Kenyan organizational cultures, there exists a deep-rooted deference to hierarchy and seniority. While respect for leadership is a cultural strength, it can inadvertently silence the voices of junior employees who possess critical on-the-ground insights. Studies on East African workplace dynamics show that organizations that actively create safe channels for upward feedback reduce error rates by up to 26% and show significantly higher innovation output.

💡 Speaking up is not disrespectful — it is your contribution.

CARD 3 OF 3

3. Four practical behaviors that build safety

Dr. Timothy Clark identifies four stages: Inclusion Safety (you belong here), Learner Safety (you can ask questions and make mistakes), Contributor Safety (you can make a difference), and Challenger Safety (you can question the status quo). As a team member, you build safety by listening without judgment, separating people from their ideas, celebrating questions rather than only answers, and never weaponizing vulnerability.

💡 Safety is built in small moments, not grand gestures.
